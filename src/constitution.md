# CONSTITUTION OF FAMILY JUSTICE MONITOR NEW ZEALAND INCORPORATED

## 1. NAME

The name of the Society is **Family Justice Monitor New Zealand Incorporated** (also referred to as "the Society" or "Family Justice Monitor NZ").

## 2. PURPOSES AND OBJECTS

The purposes of the Society are exclusively charitable and for the public benefit, being:

1. To conduct independent research, monitoring, and analysis of New Zealand's family justice system and its impacts on children, families, whānau, and communities.

2. To promote transparency and accountability within New Zealand's family justice system through evidence-based research and public education.

3. To provide a platform for families and whānau with lived experience of the family justice system to share their stories in a safe, confidential, and legally compliant manner.

4. To inform public policy, legislative reform, and professional practice relating to family justice through rigorous, independent research and analysis.

5. To advance education and awareness about the operation and impacts of the family justice system among the public, policymakers, legal professionals, and affected communities.

6. To uphold Te Tiriti o Waitangi principles and ensure Māori and Pacific peoples' perspectives are centred in the Society's work, recognising their disproportionate representation in family justice proceedings.

7. To collaborate with other organisations, researchers, and community groups working to improve outcomes for children and families within the justice system.

8. To undertake any other charitable activities ancillary or incidental to the above purposes that advance the public benefit.

## 3. POWERS

To achieve its purposes, the Society shall have the power to:

1. Conduct research using quantitative and qualitative methodologies, including surveys, interviews, case studies, and data analysis.

2. Collect, analyse, and publish statistical data and research findings relating to family justice outcomes and processes.

3. Prepare and publish reports, submissions, educational materials, and other publications.

4. Organise and participate in conferences, seminars, workshops, and public education events.

5. Engage with policymakers, government agencies, legal professionals, and community organisations.

6. Seek, receive, and administer grants, donations, bequests, and other funding from individuals, trusts, foundations, and organisations.

7. Employ staff, engage contractors and consultants, and recruit volunteers.

8. Enter into contracts, leases, and other agreements necessary for the Society's operations.

9. Acquire, hold, maintain, and dispose of real and personal property.

10. Open and maintain bank accounts and invest funds prudently.

11. Establish advisory bodies, working groups, and committees to support the Society's work.

12. Affiliate with or support other organisations with aligned purposes.

13. Do all other lawful things necessary or conducive to achieving the Society's purposes.

## 4. NOT-FOR-PROFIT STATUS

1. The Society is a not-for-profit organisation.

2. The income, benefit, and advantage of the Society shall be applied exclusively towards the promotion of the Society's purposes.

3. No member of the Society shall receive any direct or indirect financial benefit, payment, or advantage from the Society, except for:
   - Reasonable and proper remuneration for services actually rendered to the Society
   - Reimbursement of reasonable expenses properly incurred on behalf of the Society
   - Interest at a rate not exceeding current commercial rates on money lent to the Society
   - Reasonable rent for premises let to the Society

## 5. MEMBERSHIP

### 5.1 Categories of Membership

The Society shall have the following categories of membership:

1. **Founding Members**: The initial members who adopt this Constitution and establish the Society.

2. **Ordinary Members**: Persons admitted to membership following incorporation.

3. **Life Members**: Persons recognised by the Society for exceptional service or contribution.

### 5.2 Eligibility

1. Any person aged 18 years or older who supports the purposes of the Society may apply for membership.

2. Organisations (bodies corporate) may apply for membership and shall exercise their membership rights through an appointed representative.

### 5.3 Application for Membership

1. Application for membership shall be made in writing to the Committee in such form as the Committee may prescribe.

2. The Committee shall consider each application and may approve or decline the application at its discretion.

3. An applicant becomes a member upon:
   - Approval of their application by the Committee
   - Payment of any applicable membership fee
   - Provision of written consent to become a member

### 5.4 Membership Rights

Every member shall have the right to:

1. Receive notice of and attend General Meetings
2. Speak and vote at General Meetings
3. Stand for election to the Committee
4. Receive copies of the Society's annual financial statements and annual report
5. Inspect the Register of Members and Constitution

### 5.5 Membership Obligations

Every member shall:

1. Support the purposes and values of the Society
2. Comply with this Constitution and any bylaws or policies adopted by the Society
3. Pay any membership fees as determined by the Committee
4. Conduct themselves in a manner that upholds the reputation and integrity of the Society

### 5.6 Membership Fees

1. The Committee may set annual membership fees.
2. The Committee may waive or reduce fees in cases of financial hardship.
3. Membership fees are not refundable.

### 5.7 Cessation of Membership

Membership ceases when:

1. The member dies or, in the case of an organisation, is dissolved
2. The member resigns by written notice to the Society
3. The member fails to pay membership fees within three months of the due date, unless the Committee grants an extension
4. The member is removed from membership under clause 5.8
5. The Society is liquidated or deregistered

### 5.8 Removal of Members

1. The Committee may resolve to remove a member if:
   - The member has acted in a manner contrary to the purposes of the Society
   - The member has brought the Society into disrepute
   - The member has breached this Constitution or the Society's policies
   - The member's continued membership is not in the best interests of the Society

2. Before removing a member, the Committee must:
   - Provide the member with written notice of the proposed removal and the reasons
   - Give the member at least 14 days to respond in writing
   - Consider any response provided by the member
   - Allow the member to speak at a Committee meeting (in person or by video/phone)

3. A member removed by the Committee may appeal to a General Meeting by giving written notice within 14 days. The General Meeting's decision is final.

### 5.9 Register of Members

1. The Society shall maintain a Register of Members containing:
   - Each member's full name and contact details
   - Date of admission to membership
   - Category of membership
   - Date and reason for cessation of membership (if applicable)

2. The Register shall be kept at the Society's registered office or other secure location.

3. Members may inspect the Register at reasonable times upon request to the Contact Person.

## 6. GOVERNANCE STRUCTURE

### 6.1 The Committee

1. The affairs of the Society shall be governed by a Committee elected by the members.

2. The Committee shall be responsible for:
   - Strategic direction and policy development
   - Financial management and oversight
   - Ensuring compliance with legal and regulatory requirements
   - Upholding the purposes and values of the Society
   - Appointing and overseeing any staff or contractors
   - Reporting to members

### 6.2 Committee Composition

1. The Committee shall consist of no fewer than seven (7) and no more than nine (9) members, including:
   - Chairperson
   - Secretary
   - Treasurer
   - At least four (4) other Committee members

2. The Committee should strive to include diverse perspectives and expertise, including:
   - Legal expertise in family law
   - Research and methodological expertise
   - Child welfare and development expertise
   - Lived experience of the family justice system
   - Māori representation
   - Pacific peoples representation
   - Financial and operational management expertise

### 6.3 Eligibility for Committee Membership

To be eligible for election or appointment to the Committee, a person must:

1. Be a member of the Society
2. Be aged 18 years or older
3. Not be disqualified under the Incorporated Societies Act 2022
4. Not be an undischarged bankrupt
5. Not be prohibited from being a director or promoter of a company under the Companies Act 1993

### 6.4 Election of Committee Members

1. Committee members shall be elected at an Annual General Meeting.

2. Nominations for Committee positions must be:
   - Made in writing
   - Signed by the nominee indicating consent
   - Delivered to the Secretary at least seven (7) days before the Annual General Meeting

3. If the number of nominees does not exceed the number of vacancies, those nominees shall be declared elected.

4. If the number of nominees exceeds the number of vacancies, elections shall be by secret ballot.

5. Each member present at the meeting has one vote per vacancy.

### 6.5 Term of Office

1. Committee members shall hold office for a term of two (2) years.

2. Committee members may be re-elected or re-appointed for successive terms, provided that:
   - The Chairperson shall not serve more than four (4) consecutive years in that role
   - No Committee member shall serve more than six (6) consecutive years without a break of at least one (1) year

3. Committee member terms shall be staggered to ensure continuity, with approximately half the Committee elected each year.

### 6.6 Committee Officers

1. At the first Committee meeting following the Annual General Meeting, the Committee shall elect from among its members:
   - A Chairperson
   - A Secretary
   - A Treasurer

2. Officers hold office until the first Committee meeting following the next Annual General Meeting, but may be re-elected.

### 6.7 Casual Vacancies

1. The Committee may appoint a member to fill a casual vacancy on the Committee.

2. A person appointed to fill a casual vacancy holds office only until the next Annual General Meeting.

3. A casual vacancy occurs when a Committee member:
   - Dies
   - Resigns by written notice
   - Is removed from office
   - Becomes ineligible under clause 6.3
   - Is absent without leave for three consecutive Committee meetings

### 6.8 Removal of Committee Members

1. The Society in a General Meeting may, by resolution passed by a two-thirds majority of members present and voting, remove any Committee member before the end of their term.

2. The Committee member must be given written notice and an opportunity to be heard at the General Meeting before any resolution is voted upon.

### 6.9 Committee Meetings

1. The Committee shall meet at least four (4) times per year.

2. The Chairperson or any three Committee members may call a Committee meeting by giving at least seven (7) days' written notice.

3. In cases of urgency, a Committee meeting may be called on shorter notice if all Committee members consent.

4. Notice of Committee meetings must include the date, time, location (or details for remote attendance), and agenda.

5. Committee meetings may be held in person, by video conference, telephone, or other electronic means.

6. A quorum for Committee meetings shall be half the number of Committee members (rounded up to the nearest whole number).

7. If a quorum is not present within 30 minutes of the scheduled time, the meeting shall be adjourned to a time and date determined by the Chairperson.

### 6.10 Committee Decisions

1. Decisions of the Committee shall be made by majority vote.

2. Each Committee member has one vote.

3. The Chairperson has a deliberative vote and, in the case of an equality of votes, a casting vote.

4. The Committee may make decisions by circular resolution without a meeting if all Committee members consent in writing (including by email).

### 6.11 Committee Authority

1. The Committee may:
   - Delegate specific tasks or functions to subcommittees, staff, or contractors
   - Establish advisory bodies, working groups, or expert panels
   - Develop and adopt policies and procedures for the Society's operations
   - Make decisions on all matters not specifically reserved for General Meetings

2. The Committee shall not delegate its responsibility for:
   - Strategic oversight and governance
   - Financial management and accountability
   - Compliance with legal and constitutional requirements
   - Employment of staff or engagement of contractors

## 7. DUTIES OF COMMITTEE OFFICERS

### 7.1 Chairperson

The Chairperson shall:

1. Provide leadership to the Committee and the Society
2. Chair Committee meetings and General Meetings
3. Ensure the Committee functions effectively
4. Act as a spokesperson for the Society (or delegate this function)
5. Ensure that decisions of the Committee and General Meetings are implemented
6. Maintain oversight of the Society's strategic direction

### 7.2 Secretary

The Secretary shall:

1. Maintain accurate records of Committee meetings and General Meetings
2. Ensure proper notice is given for all meetings
3. Maintain the Register of Members
4. Conduct correspondence on behalf of the Society as directed by the Committee
5. Ensure compliance with filing requirements under the Incorporated Societies Act 2022
6. Have custody of the Society's Constitution and records

### 7.3 Treasurer

The Treasurer shall:

1. Ensure proper financial records are kept
2. Present financial reports to Committee meetings and General Meetings
3. Oversee the preparation of annual financial statements
4. Ensure that all financial obligations are met
5. Provide financial advice to the Committee
6. Arrange for the audit or review of financial statements as required

## 8. CONTACT PERSON

1. The Society shall have at least one Contact Person as required by the Incorporated Societies Act 2022.

2. The Secretary shall be the Contact Person unless the Committee appoints another person.

3. The Contact Person shall be responsible for receiving communications from the Registrar of Incorporated Societies and ensuring the Committee is informed.

4. The Contact Person must be ordinarily resident in New Zealand.

## 9. GENERAL MEETINGS

### 9.1 Annual General Meeting

1. The Society shall hold an Annual General Meeting once in every calendar year, within six (6) months of the end of the financial year.

2. The business of the Annual General Meeting shall include:
   - Receiving and considering the annual report from the Committee
   - Receiving and considering the annual financial statements
   - Receiving any auditor's or reviewer's report
   - Electing Committee members
   - Transacting any other business of which notice has been given

### 9.2 Special General Meetings

1. A Special General Meeting may be called by:
   - The Committee
   - The Chairperson
   - At least 20% of members by written requisition to the Committee

2. If a Special General Meeting is requisitioned by members, the Committee must call the meeting within 30 days of receiving the requisition.

### 9.3 Notice of General Meetings

1. At least 14 days' written notice must be given to all members for any General Meeting.

2. Notice must include:
   - The date, time, and location of the meeting (or details for remote attendance)
   - The nature of the business to be transacted
   - Any proposed resolutions

3. Notice may be given by post, email, or other electronic means.

4. Accidental omission to give notice to any member does not invalidate the proceedings of a General Meeting.

### 9.4 Proceedings at General Meetings

1. General Meetings may be held in person, by video conference, telephone, or other electronic means that allows members to participate.

2. A quorum for General Meetings shall be 10 members or one-third of members, whichever is fewer.

3. If a quorum is not present within 30 minutes of the scheduled time:
   - If requisitioned by members, the meeting shall be dissolved
   - In any other case, the meeting shall be adjourned to the same time and place one week later, and if a quorum is not present at the adjourned meeting, those present shall constitute a quorum

4. The Chairperson shall chair all General Meetings. If the Chairperson is absent, the meeting shall elect a chairperson for that meeting.

5. The chairperson of the meeting may:
   - Adjourn the meeting with the consent of the meeting
   - Direct the manner of voting
   - Maintain order and exclude any person who is disrupting the meeting

### 9.5 Voting at General Meetings

1. Each member has one vote.

2. Voting shall be by show of hands, voice vote, or electronic poll unless a secret ballot is requested.

3. A secret ballot may be requested by:
   - The chairperson of the meeting
   - At least five (5) members present

4. In the case of an equality of votes, the chairperson of the meeting has a casting vote in addition to their deliberative vote.

5. Resolutions shall be passed by simple majority except where this Constitution requires a special majority.

6. Members may cast votes:
   - In person or by electronic participation
   - By postal vote if authorised by the Committee
   - By proxy in accordance with clause 9.6

### 9.6 Proxies

1. A member may appoint another member as their proxy to attend and vote on their behalf at a General Meeting.

2. Proxy appointments must:
   - Be in writing
   - Be signed by the appointing member
   - Be delivered to the Secretary at least 48 hours before the meeting

3. A proxy form must specify the meeting for which it is given.

4. A proxy may not speak at a meeting except to vote.

5. A member acting as proxy may hold no more than two (2) proxies.

## 10. FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT

### 10.1 Financial Year

The financial year of the Society shall run from 1 July to 30 June, or such other dates as the Committee may determine.

### 10.2 Financial Records

1. The Society shall keep accurate financial records that:
   - Correctly record and explain all transactions and financial position
   - Enable true and fair financial statements to be prepared
   - Comply with the Incorporated Societies Act 2022 and any other applicable legislation

2. Financial records shall be kept for at least seven (7) years.

### 10.3 Bank Accounts

1. The Society shall maintain one or more bank accounts in the Society's name.

2. All payments must be authorised by at least two Committee members, one of whom should be the Treasurer or Chairperson.

3. The Committee may establish different authorisation procedures for payments below a specified threshold.

### 10.4 Financial Statements

1. The Committee shall ensure that annual financial statements are prepared in accordance with applicable accounting standards.

2. Financial statements shall be:
   - Submitted to the Annual General Meeting
   - Filed with the Registrar of Incorporated Societies
   - Made available to members upon request

### 10.5 Audit or Review

1. If required by the Incorporated Societies Act 2022, the Society's financial statements shall be audited or reviewed.

2. The Annual General Meeting may appoint an auditor or reviewer.

3. An auditor or reviewer must be qualified and independent.

### 10.6 Funding and Donations

1. The Society may seek funding from individuals, trusts, foundations, government agencies, and other sources.

2. The Society shall maintain a register of all significant donations and grants, including conditions attached.

3. The Committee shall ensure transparency regarding funding sources and shall disclose any actual or potential conflicts of interest.

4. The Society shall not accept funding that would compromise its independence or conflict with its purposes and values.

## 11. CONFLICT OF INTEREST

### 11.1 Disclosure

1. Committee members must promptly disclose to the Committee any actual or potential conflict of interest.

2. A conflict of interest includes any situation where a Committee member's personal interests, or the interests of a person or organisation they are associated with, could influence or appear to influence their decisions on behalf of the Society.

### 11.2 Managing Conflicts

1. A Committee member with a conflict of interest in a matter:
   - Must not vote on the matter
   - Must not participate in discussions unless invited by the Committee to provide information
   - Must not be present during voting unless the Committee resolves otherwise

2. The conflict and how it was managed must be recorded in the minutes.

## 12. AMENDMENT OF CONSTITUTION

### 12.1 Procedure

1. This Constitution may be amended, added to, or revoked by resolution at a General Meeting, provided that:
   - At least 14 days' written notice of the proposed amendment is given to all members
   - The notice includes the text of the proposed amendment
   - The resolution is passed by at least two-thirds of members present and voting

2. Any amendment to the Constitution must be filed with the Registrar of Incorporated Societies within 20 working days.

3. An amendment takes effect from the date of the General Meeting or such later date as the resolution specifies, unless the Registrar directs otherwise.

### 12.2 Restrictions

1. No amendment shall be made that would:
   - Cause the Society to cease to be an incorporated society
   - Allow the distribution of income, benefit, or advantage to members
   - Be inconsistent with the Incorporated Societies Act 2022
   - Conflict with charitable status or tax-exemption requirements (if applicable)

## 13. DISPUTE RESOLUTION

### 13.1 Disputes Between Members

1. Any dispute between members, or between members and the Society, should first be addressed through direct communication and good faith negotiation.

2. If the dispute cannot be resolved informally, either party may refer the matter to the Committee.

3. The Committee shall:
   - Hear from all parties involved
   - Attempt to facilitate a resolution
   - Make a decision or recommendation within 30 days

4. If the dispute is not resolved to the satisfaction of the parties, it may be referred to mediation.

### 13.2 Mediation

1. If a dispute is referred to mediation, the parties shall:
   - Agree on a mediator within 14 days
   - If they cannot agree, the mediator shall be appointed by the President of the New Zealand Law Society or their nominee
   - Share the costs of mediation equally unless otherwise agreed

2. Mediation shall be conducted in accordance with the mediator's standard procedures.

3. The decision to proceed to mediation does not prevent a party from exercising their legal rights if mediation is unsuccessful.

### 13.3 Complaints

1. Any person may make a complaint about the conduct of the Society or its members by submitting a written complaint to the Committee.

2. The Committee shall acknowledge receipt of the complaint within seven (7) days.

3. The Committee shall investigate and respond to the complaint within 30 days, or inform the complainant if more time is required.

4. If the complaint involves a Committee member, that member shall not participate in any decisions relating to the complaint.

## 14. COMMON SEAL (OPTIONAL)

1. The Society may have a common seal.

2. If the Society has a common seal, it shall only be used by authority of the Committee.

3. The affixing of the seal must be witnessed by two Committee members.

## 15. DISSOLUTION AND DISTRIBUTION OF ASSETS

### 15.1 Voluntary Liquidation

1. The Society may be voluntarily liquidated by resolution at a Special General Meeting called for that purpose, provided that:
   - At least 20 working days' notice of the meeting and the proposed resolution is given to all members
   - The resolution is passed by at least 75% of members entitled to vote and voting

2. The Society must comply with all requirements of the Incorporated Societies Act 2022 regarding liquidation.

### 15.2 Distribution of Surplus Assets

1. Upon liquidation or deregistration of the Society, after payment of all debts and liabilities, any surplus assets shall be distributed to one or more organisations that:
   - Have purposes similar to those of the Society
   - Are charitable organisations registered under the Charities Act 2005 (if the Society is registered as a charity)
   - Prohibit the distribution of income, benefit, or advantage to members

2. The distribution of surplus assets shall be determined by resolution at the Special General Meeting that resolves to liquidate the Society.

3. If no determination is made, the surplus assets shall be distributed to such organisations as the High Court may direct.

4. In no circumstances shall surplus assets be distributed to members of the Society.

## 16. INDEMNITY AND INSURANCE

### 16.1 Indemnity

Subject to the Incorporated Societies Act 2022, the Society may indemnify a Committee member, employee, or volunteer for any liability or costs incurred in connection with their role, except to the extent caused by their own wrongful act or omission.

### 16.2 Insurance

The Society may effect insurance for Committee members, employees, or volunteers for any liability or costs for which the Society may indemnify them.

## 17. MISCELLANEOUS PROVISIONS

### 17.1 Bylaws and Policies

1. The Committee may make, amend, and revoke bylaws and policies for the governance and operation of the Society, provided they are consistent with this Constitution and the Incorporated Societies Act 2022.

2. Bylaws and policies shall be made available to members.

### 17.2 Legal Compliance

1. The Society shall comply with all applicable laws, including:
   - Incorporated Societies Act 2022
   - Charities Act 2005 (if registered as a charity)
   - Privacy Act 2020
   - Health and Safety at Work Act 2015
   - Family Courts Act 1980 (particularly Section 11B regarding publication restrictions)

2. The Committee shall ensure appropriate policies and procedures are in place to ensure compliance.

### 17.3 Interpretation

1. In this Constitution, unless the context requires otherwise:
   - "Committee" means the governing body of the Society elected or appointed under this Constitution
   - "Financial year" means the accounting period of the Society
   - "General Meeting" means an Annual General Meeting or Special General Meeting
   - "Member" means a member of the Society
   - "In writing" includes email and other electronic communication
   - "Working day" has the meaning set out in the Incorporated Societies Act 2022

2. The headings in this Constitution are for convenience only and do not affect its interpretation.

3. Any matters not provided for in this Constitution shall be determined in accordance with the Incorporated Societies Act 2022.

### 17.4 Transitional Provisions

Until the first Annual General Meeting after incorporation:

1. The founding members may act as the Committee
2. The founding members shall elect interim officers (Chairperson, Secretary, and Treasurer)
3. The Committee may co-opt additional members if required to meet the minimum Committee size

## NOTES FOR IMPLEMENTATION

This Constitution has been drafted to comply with the Incorporated Societies Act 2022 and to position the organisation for charitable registration. Before adopting and filing this Constitution:

1. **Complete the signature table** with at least 10 founding members
2. **Confirm contact details** for the registered office and contact person
3. **Review with legal counsel** to ensure alignment with current legislation and charitable registration requirements
4. **Hold a founding meeting** to adopt the Constitution and elect the initial Committee
5. **File application for incorporation** with the Registrar of Incorporated Societies within required timeframes
6. **Develop supporting policies** for operations, including:
   - Health and Safety policy
   - Privacy policy
   - Conflict of Interest policy
   - Financial Management policy
   - Research Ethics policy
7. **Apply for charitable registration** once incorporated (if seeking charitable status)
