Media Coverage of the Family Court

How New Zealand news outlets report on the family justice system — categorised and assessed for sentiment, article by article.

The family court system rarely speaks for itself in public; most New Zealanders encounter it through what gets reported. This page tracks that reporting directly: every article below has been read and classified by a human reviewer against a fixed set of categories, so patterns in coverage — what gets covered, how it's framed, and towards whom — become visible over time rather than anecdotal.

This is the first output of the research approach described in our Media Curator role: systematically collecting and cataloguing family court coverage rather than relying on impression or selective examples.

Methodology

Discovery: Articles are surfaced from New Zealand news RSS feeds and targeted news searches for family-court-related terms, run on a recurring schedule.

Relevance screening: A human reviewer first confirms whether each article is genuinely about the New Zealand family court system before any further tagging occurs — articles that merely contain a matching keyword but aren't actually relevant (e.g. unrelated overseas stories) are excluded from this page entirely rather than folded into a miscellaneous category.

Categorisation: Relevant articles are classified by category, overall sentiment, and stance toward the court system specifically, plus (for domestic violence and protection order coverage) the alleged perpetrator's and victim's gender as reported.

Independence: Tagging is performed by a human reviewer against a fixed set of categories, not selected to support a predetermined conclusion. As the volunteer Media Curator programme grows, we intend to publish inter-rater agreement figures once more than one reviewer is tagging articles.
